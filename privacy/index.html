Mint

Privacy Policy

Effective October 12, 2026

Summary

Mint has no advertising and no analytics. Your vocabulary list, progress and settings stay on your device. An account is optional and is used only for the AI features. The only content that leaves your device is what the AI features need: the topic, word or sentence you ask about, your language pair and your level.

Data stored on your device

Your vocabulary packs, the words you add, your learning progress, review schedule, streak and settings are stored on your device, in storage shared between the app and its widgets. Mint does not upload this data. Deleting the app deletes it.

Your account

The AI features ask you to sign in with Apple or with Google. Mint asks them for no name and no email address. If you sign in with Google, you do so on Google's own page, and Google learns that you use Mint. Mint's server keeps: an identifier derived from the anonymous user ID that Apple or Google gives this app; for each device you sign in on, the public part of a key that shows a request comes from the genuine app, together with that key's identifier, Apple's receipt for it, a usage counter and the times it was created and last used; short-lived sign-in sessions; and one record for each AI request, saying which feature was used and when, with a fingerprint of the request that lets the server recognise a repeat, but without its content. The result of an AI request is kept for up to 24 hours, so that a repeated request is not charged twice, and is then erased. The request records are kept for 30 days. Mint's server does not keep your vocabulary list or your progress. Like any internet service, Mint's server and its hosting provider, Cloudflare, can see your IP address. On your device, the app keeps the key's identifier and, if you signed in with Apple, Apple's user ID in the Keychain; these can remain after you delete the app and are replaced the next time you sign in. The app keeps nothing from Google on your device.

Signing out and deleting your account

You can sign out or delete your account in Settings. Signing out removes the sign-in from this device and, when the server can be reached, unlinks the device there and ends its sessions; the server forgets a device it has unlinked 30 days later. If the server could not be reached, the device stays linked there until you delete the account, or sign in on that device again and then sign out while online. Signing out erases nothing else. Deleting erases the account, the devices signed in to it and any stored results straight away, and asks Apple or Google, whichever you signed in with, to remove Mint from your account there. If that does not succeed, the app tells you how to remove Mint yourself. The request records described above remain for up to 30 days, without content, so that deleting an account and coming back does not reset the daily limit. If an account was blocked for misuse, a marker of that block is kept.

AI generation

When you ask Mint to create packs, words, translations or example sentences, the app sends the request to Mint's server, which passes it to DeepSeek, a third-party AI provider. The request contains the topic or pack name, the words or phrases involved, your native and learning languages and your level. DeepSeek is not sent your account identifier. Mint's server keeps the generated result for up to 24 hours, so that a request interrupted by a lost connection can be completed without being generated twice, and then deletes it. DeepSeek processes the request on its servers, which may be outside your country, under its own privacy policy. Do not put personal or confidential information into topics, pack names or words.

Pack catalog

The catalog of ready-made packs is downloaded from a public file host (GitHub). As with any internet request, the host can see your IP address.

Notifications and speech

Reminders are scheduled on your device and are only sent if you turn them on. Pronunciation uses the speech voices built into your device.

Tracking

Mint does not track you across apps or websites and does not share data with advertisers or data brokers.

Children

Mint is a general-audience app and does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published in the app with a new effective date.

This is the same policy the Mint app shows under Settings.
